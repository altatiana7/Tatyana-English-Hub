Reading

Text on the left, tasks on the right — compact, no unnecessary stretching.

Elections involve several stages. Before election day, candidates present their ideas and voters receive information about the issues being discussed. On election day, eligible voters go to polling stations, receive ballot papers and cast their votes. Voting procedures are designed to keep individual choices private. After polling stations close, ballots are counted and results are announced. Voter turnout is used to describe the proportion of eligible voters who took part. A referendum differs from an election because citizens vote directly on a specific question rather than choosing a representative. The vocabulary connected with elections is useful in news reports, formal discussions and exam tasks because it allows speakers to describe the process accurately without expressing support for a particular political choice.
