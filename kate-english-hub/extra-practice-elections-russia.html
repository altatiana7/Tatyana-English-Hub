Reading

Read and answer without translating every word.

Elections involve several stages. Before election day, candidates present their ideas and voters receive information about the issues being discussed. On election day, eligible voters go to polling stations, receive ballot papers and cast their votes. Voting is normally designed to protect the secrecy of individual choices. After the polling stations close, ballots are counted and results are announced. Voter turnout is often used to describe the proportion of eligible voters who took part. A referendum is different from an election because citizens vote directly on a specific question rather than choosing a representative.
