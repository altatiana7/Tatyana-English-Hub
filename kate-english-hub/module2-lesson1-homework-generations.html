Homework Tasks

1. Generation Facts & Profiles

Baby Boomers
1946–1964
Grew up before the Internet. Stability and long-term employment are often associated with this group.
Generation X
1965–1980
Experienced both analogue and digital life and is often associated with independence and adaptability.
Millennials
1981–1996
Came of age during rapid Internet growth and are often linked with flexibility and work-life balance.
Generation Z
1997–2012
Digital natives who often prefer messaging, short-form content and fast access to information.
Generation Alpha
2013–present
Growing up with AI, touchscreens and increasingly personalised digital education.

4. Short Reading

People often talk about Baby Boomers, Gen X, Millennials, Gen Z and Gen Alpha as if each generation had one personality. In reality, generational labels are only a rough guide. Historical events, technology, education and economic conditions can influence people, but family background and personal experience matter too. Younger workers may be more willing to change jobs for flexibility or meaningful work, while older workers may place more value on long-term stability. Communication habits can also differ: some people prefer phone calls, while others prefer messaging. These differences can be useful to discuss, but they become misleading when they turn into stereotypes.
