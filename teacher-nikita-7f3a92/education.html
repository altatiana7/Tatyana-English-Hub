1. Unexpected Facts — True or False?

Choose first. Open the answer only after Nikita explains his reasoning.

1. Voting

Austria allows citizens to vote in national elections from the age of 16.

TRUE. Austria lowered the voting age to 16 for nationwide elections in 2007 and was the first country in Europe to do so for all elections.

RU: Правда. В Австрии голосовать на общенациональных выборах можно с 16 лет.
Ask: “Does a lower voting age necessarily mean younger voters are less informed?”

2. Prison + Education

There are school departments in every Norwegian prison.

TRUE. A 2025 Norwegian government report states that there are school departments in all Norwegian prisons.

RU: Правда. В норвежских тюрьмах есть школьные подразделения.
Ask: “Should education in prison be treated as a right, a privilege, or a rehabilitation tool?”

3. Prison + Rights

In Norway, prisoners can lose their right to basic education simply because they are serving a sentence.

FALSE. Norwegian guidance says prisoners have rights to primary and upper-secondary education on the same basis as the general population, within the limits of imprisonment.

RU: Неправда. Сам факт заключения не отменяет право на базовое образование.
Useful phrase: retain the right to education — сохранять право на образование.

4. Religion + Knowledge

People who personally know someone from another religion tend to know less about that religion.

FALSE. In a Pew survey, people with more religiously diverse social networks generally knew more about religion. For example, those who personally knew a Muslim were much more likely to identify Ramadan correctly.

RU: Неправда. Более разнообразный круг общения был связан с лучшими знаниями о религиях.
Vocabulary: religiously diverse social network — религиозно разнообразный круг общения.

5. Religion + Education

Taking a class about world religions is associated with higher factual knowledge about religion.

TRUE. In Pew’s 2019 U.S. survey, people who had taken a world-religions class answered more factual religion questions correctly on average than those who had not.

RU: Правда. Участники, изучавшие мировые религии, в среднем лучше отвечали на фактические вопросы о религии.
Bridge: “So education can change not only what we know, but also what assumptions we make.”

6. Evidence

If a statistic is factually correct, it cannot be misleading.

FALSE. A statistic can be technically correct but misleading if the sample is selective, key cases are missing, or the denominator/context is hidden.

RU: Неправда. Цифра может быть формально верной, но вводить в заблуждение из-за выборки или отсутствующего контекста.
Key phrase: technically true but misleading.

Vocabulary next

EN: “We already need words like evidence, sample, assumption and misleading. Let’s train that language before we start the main discussion.”

«Нам уже понадобились слова evidence, sample, assumption, misleading. Сначала натренируем эту лексику, а потом пойдём в основное обсуждение.»

EN: “Now that we have the language, let’s use it to connect the bigger ideas: what actually shapes the way people think and behave?”

«Прежде чем говорить об образовании, давай посмотрим шире: что вообще формирует то, как люди думают и ведут себя?» Логика: от общей темы урока → к институтам и обществу.

7. Grammar Lab — modal perfects

Use the film to reconstruct the past and judge decisions.

must have + V3

Strong deduction about the past.

The cellmate must have seen this happen before.

must have + V3 = почти уверенный вывод о прошлом: «должно быть / наверняка». Мы не видели событие, но по фактам делаем сильный вывод. Пример: He must have known. — Он, должно быть, знал.
might / may have + V3

Possible explanation.

He might have thought the box was harmless.

might/may have + V3 = возможное объяснение прошлого: «возможно, он…». Уверенность ниже, чем с must have. Пример: He might have misunderstood. — Возможно, он неправильно понял.
could have + V3

Alternative possibility / missed option.

He could have left the box alone.

could have + V3 = вариант, который был возможен в прошлом, но не произошёл: «мог бы». Пример: He could have walked away. — Он мог бы уйти.
should / shouldn’t have + V3

Criticism or regret.

He shouldn’t have ignored the warning.

should have + V3 = «следовало сделать», shouldn’t have + V3 = «не следовало делать». Используем для критики или сожаления о прошлом.
can’t have + V3

Strong negative deduction.

The box can’t have been harmless.

can’t have + V3 = сильный отрицательный вывод: «не мог / не могло быть». Пример: He can’t have known everything.

Transform the ideas

  1. The cellmate probably knew the danger. → must have
  2. Perhaps the prisoner misunderstood the warning. → might have
  3. He had another option: leave the box closed. → could have
  4. Opening the box was a bad decision. → shouldn’t have
  5. Maybe the system was designed to trap curious people. → might have
1. The cellmate must have known the danger. 2. The prisoner might have misunderstood the warning. 3. He could have left the box closed. 4. He shouldn’t have opened the box. 5. The system might have been designed to trap curious people.

EN: “We’ve used grammar to reconstruct one person’s decision. Now let’s ask a bigger question: can whole groups make bad decisions because they are looking at incomplete evidence?”

«Мы восстановили решение одного человека. Теперь вопрос шире: могут ли целые группы ошибаться, потому что смотрят на неполные данные?» Логика: личное решение → когнитивная ошибка и данные.

8. Survivorship Bias — What are we missing?

Start with the evidence. Do not reveal the term immediately.

Mystery case — You are the analyst

During WWII, analysts studied planes that returned from combat. Many had bullet holes in the wings and fuselage, but very few around the engines and cockpit. Where should extra armour be added?
What about the planes that never came back?
The returning aircraft are only the survivors. Hits to areas with few holes on returning planes may have been fatal.
Для тебя: сначала не называй «ошибку выжившего». Пусть Никита сам выберет, где усиливать броню. Потом спроси: Which group is missing from the data? — «Какой группы не хватает в данных?»

Definition

Survivorship bias is the tendency to focus on successful or visible cases while overlooking those that failed, disappeared or were excluded.

Ошибка выжившего — мы смотрим на тех, кто «дошёл до финиша», и забываем о тех, кто исчез из выборки. Поэтому данные могут казаться убедительными, хотя картина неполная.

Core question

Which group is missing from the data?

Use this question in every case below.

Case A — University

“Several famous entrepreneurs dropped out of university and became extremely successful. Therefore, university is unnecessary.”

Visible group
successful dropouts
Missing group
dropouts who struggled or failed
Better conclusion
success stories alone are not enough
EN: “The sample is not representative. We hear about exceptional dropouts, but not about people who left university and later struggled. University may still improve average outcomes.”

RU: «Выборка нерепрезентативна. Мы слышим об исключительных успешных случаях, но не о тех, кто бросил вуз и столкнулся с трудностями. Университет всё равно может улучшать средние шансы.»

Case B — Language learning

“Ten people became fluent in a foreign language through self-study. Therefore, self-study is the best method.”

Visible
successful self-learners
Missing
people who used the same method but quit

Possible EN: “Motivation, previous language experience, contact with native speakers, time available and the learner’s social environment may all have influenced the outcome.”

Case C — Prison rehabilitation

“These former prisoners completed education programmes and now have successful careers. Therefore, prison education always prevents reoffending.”

Visible
successful former prisoners
Missing

Check

Case D — Social media

“Every successful productivity influencer I follow wakes up at 5 a.m. Therefore, waking up at 5 makes people successful.”

Upgrade: correlation does not necessarily mean causation.

Correlation ≠ causation: два явления могут встречаться вместе, но одно не обязательно является причиной другого. Ранний подъём может сопровождать успех, но не доказывает, что именно он его вызвал.
EN: “Waking up early may be associated with a certain lifestyle, but it does not prove causation. Discipline, income, health, job type and personal routines may be confounding factors.”

RU: «Ранний подъём может быть связан с определённым образом жизни, но не доказывает причинность. Дисциплина, доход, здоровье, тип работы и привычки могут быть дополнительными факторами.»

Modal Perfects — use the bias cases

  • The analysts must have focused on the planes that returned.
  • They might have overlooked the missing aircraft.
  • They could have reached a different conclusion.
  • They should have considered the planes that never returned.
  • The returned planes can’t have represented the whole population.

EN: “The facts already gave us the problem. Now let’s learn the language we’ll need to explain it precisely.”

«Факты уже показали нам проблему. Теперь выучим язык, который понадобится, чтобы точно её объяснить.» Логика: True/False → vocabulary → discussion.
