ART SECRETS · 7–8 MIN

Quick Detective Quiz · Look, guess, reveal

Every question has the actual artwork. Maya must look first, make a prediction, and only then reveal the story.

1 · Girl with a Pearl Earring · Vermeer

Detective question: Is this a portrait of a real, identified girl?

Secret: It is a tronie — a study of a character or type, not a conventional commissioned portrait. We do not know who the girl was, or even whether the figure represents one specific person.

Look closer: the famous “pearl” is mainly created with just a few light brushstrokes. The Mauritshuis also notes that it is probably too large to be a real pearl; it may have been glass or imagined.

Extra: the dark background originally contained more visible greenish material, and Vermeer used costly ultramarine in the headscarf.

2 · American Gothic · Grant Wood

Detective question: Husband and wife?

Secret: Grant Wood intended them as a farmer and his unmarried daughter, not husband and wife.

The models: Wood used his sister Nan Wood Graham and his dentist Dr B. H. McKeeby.

The house: Wood saw a real Carpenter Gothic house in Eldon, Iowa, and imagined the kind of people who might live there.

Bonus: the painting debuted at the Art Institute of Chicago in 1930 and won a $300 prize.

3 · The Scream · Edvard Munch

Detective question: Is there only ONE original version?

Secret: The Scream is a motif, not one single object. Munch produced four colourful versions — two paintings and two drawings — and also made a lithograph.

Story: the motif grew from an experience Munch described after walking with friends at sunset and feeling a powerful “scream in nature.”

Look closer: the two people in the background continue walking while the foreground figure reacts intensely — that distance is part of the image's tension.

4 · The Persistence of Memory · Salvador Dalí

Detective question: How big do you think it is?

Secret: it is surprisingly small — only about 24.1 × 33 cm.

Melting clocks: Dalí famously connected the soft watches with soft cheese, referring to the “Camembert of time.”

Look closer: ants appear on one watch and are associated with decay; the strange soft form in the centre resembles a distorted profile of Dalí himself. The distant cliffs refer to the Catalan coast.

5 · The Arnolfini Portrait · Jan van Eyck

Detective question: How many people can you actually find?

Secret: the convex mirror reflects two additional figures in the doorway, so there are four visible people if you count the reflections.

Look above the mirror: Van Eyck wrote the equivalent of “Jan van Eyck was here. 1434.”

Money clue: the oranges were expensive in northern Europe and signal wealth. The National Gallery also points out that the room looks convincing but contains spatial inconsistencies.
