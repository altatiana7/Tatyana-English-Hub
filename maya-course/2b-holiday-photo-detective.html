04 · Behind the Photo

That's me in the picture! In 1972, Jane was living in London. She was working for an advertising agency when she met a young Frenchman, and they fell in love. Later she moved to France, where she worked at UNESCO. On Sundays the couple went to the zoo, and she often fed the animals. One weekend, they went for a walk in a Paris park. While they were walking, an owl escaped from the zoo. It also escaped a photographer's camera. The photographer was looking for pictures for a magazine. Years later, Jane's boyfriend found the photograph, and the two met the photographer.

A moment in history. On 4 November 2008, photographer Tom Pilston arrived in Chicago to photograph Barack Obama and his family. He couldn't enter the park because he didn't have a pass. He walked outside and waited for the announcement. A huge crowd was watching the results. When it became clear that Obama had won, people started celebrating, hugging and crying. The photographer captured their faces while everyone was waiting for Obama to speak.

05 · Grammar Lab · Past Simple or Past Continuous

While the crowd was waiting, the photographer took a picture.
Past ContinuousPast Simple
was / were + verb-ingverb-ed / irregular form
an action in progress, the backgrounda finished action, the main event
She was living in London.She met a Frenchman.
while + past continuouswhen + past simple
I was / he was — you / we / they were.  ·  No continuous with know, like, want, understand: I knew him (not was knowing).  ·  Two finished actions one after another — both Past Simple.

06 · Photo Trial

Tell a story the photo doesn't reveal. Choose Paris or Chicago. Describe what was happening, what happened suddenly, and what happened next. Add two details that the camera could not capture.
1. Set the scene: where, when, who?
2. Use two Past Continuous clauses.
3. Use three Past Simple actions.
4. Include two collocations from the Vocabulary Lab.
5. Give the photo a surprising title.
In November 2008, a crowd was waiting in Chicago. People were watching the results when they heard the news. Some began crying while others were celebrating. A photographer took a picture of their faces. They were looking at something important that the camera couldn't show.
Take one personal photo and write 80–100 words explaining the story behind it. Use when / while and at least five phrases from the Vocabulary Lab.
