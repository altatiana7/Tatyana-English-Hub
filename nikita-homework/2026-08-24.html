Tim Doner (BE) only thirteen when he first (BECOME) seriously interested in languages. Before that, he (STUDY) French and Latin at school, but he (NOT FEEL) particularly passionate about language learning. His attitude (CHANGE) when he (START) learning Hebrew. While he (LEARN) new languages, Tim (USE) films, TV programmes, music and conversations with native speakers. CASE 1 · THE PHANTOM WHO NEVER EXISTEDWord Formation

For more than fifteen years, police in Germany, Austria and France were involved in a huge (INVESTIGATE). The same female DNA profile appeared at many different crime scenes, so detectives believed one dangerous (OFFEND) was responsible. The DNA looked like strong forensic (EVIDENT), but there was no clear (CONNECT) between many of the crimes. Eventually, investigators made a surprising (DISCOVER): the mysterious culprit did not exist. The cotton swabs had been affected by accidental (CONTAMINATE). The case showed that even scientific evidence can be (RELY) if it is not collected and preserved (CARE).

CASE 2 · AN INNOCENT MAN ON DEATH ROWWord Formation

In 1991, Ray Krone was accused of murder in Arizona. During the trial, experts said that bite-mark (EVIDENT) linked him to the crime. The jury reached a guilty (VERDICT), and Krone received a death (SENTENCE). Years later, new DNA tests showed that the biological material at the crime scene belonged to another man. This new (EVIDENT) changed the case completely. Krone's wrongful (CONVICT) was overturned, and he was finally proved (INNOCENCE). His case showed that even forensic evidence can be (RELY) and that a suspect should not be treated as the real (CULPRIT) until guilt has been proved beyond reasonable doubt.
