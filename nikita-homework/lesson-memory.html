Background for the teacher (checked): Loftus and Palmer (1974): the verb in the question (“smashed” vs “hit”) changed speed estimates, and a week later more people in the “smashed” group said they had seen broken glass that was not there. Loftus and Pickrell (1995): 24 participants, about a quarter accepted a false “lost in a mall” memory; a 2023 replication (123 people) found about 35% judged to have a false memory but only 14% who said they really remembered it, so the effect is real but smaller than often claimed. Innocence Project: 69% of 375 DNA exonerations involved eyewitness misidentification. Case: North Carolina, 1984, a victim identified Ronald Cotton from a line-up; he served about 10.5 years; DNA excluded him and matched another man; charges were dismissed on 30 June 1995. Keep the crime itself out of the lesson: talk only about the identification.

1 · Warm-up

Interview · answer in 40 seconds
Agree, disagree or not sure?
Teacher: you read the question aloud, Nikita answers; tap the circle for 40 seconds. The question text is on the back of the card only. The poll has no right answer: ask “Why do you think so?”. Aim: lead to the idea that a sincere memory can still be wrong.

2 · Words and phrases

Cards 1–12: twelve words about memory and evidence.
Cards 13–18: six phrases about proof and doubt.
Say the word, explain it in your own words, then turn the card.
Teacher: Nikita says the word and gives the meaning in his own words first, then flips. The cards are silent: read each word aloud and ask him to repeat. Cards 13–18 are the six phrases. If time is short, take the six hardest words.

3 · Matching

Match the word and the meaning
Teacher: three rounds of four pairs, a correct pair turns green. If Nikita mixes up expressions, go back to the cards on the left and ask for his own example.

4 · Sorting · how strong is the evidence?

One possible grouping, not the only key. Stronger: DNA, CCTV, phone record, a statement written within an hour, a signed dated document. Weaker: two seconds at night, a long interrogation, a photo seen in the news, a memory years later, a far-away witness. Depends: a confident witness, fingerprints. Ask: “What would make it weaker?”. Accept reasoned alternatives.

5 · Floor is lava

A wrong button turns into lava and Nikita tries another one. If a sentence needs three tries, return to the card in block 2. Aim: 12 out of 12.

6 · Phrasal verbs for investigators

Cards on the left, gaps on the right. Nikita says the meaning in his own words first. After the quiz ask him for his own sentence with two of the verbs.

7 · Word formation

Continuous text as on the FIPI site, the root word is on the right. Nikita says the form aloud, then taps the gap. Answers: unreliable, choice, confidently, frequency, analysis, innocence, accurate, mistaken, scepticism, independent. Notice the suffixes -ence, -ency, -ly, -sis, -ism. The facts come from the Loftus and Palmer experiment and the 1984 North Carolina case (see the background on the first screen).

8 · Evidence detective · must, might, can’t, should have

must have + V3
strong inference from evidence
might / could have + V3
a possibility, the evidence is not enough
can’t have + V3
strong inference that it was impossible or very unlikely
should have + V3
criticism or regret about what was not done
Careful: do not use must have as proof of what someone remembers or intends.
must have = strong conclusion from evidence; might have = possibility; can’t have = almost certainly impossible; should have = criticism or regret. After each answer ask which piece of evidence decides it. Do not accept must have as proof of what a person remembers or intends.

9 · Error hunter

One wrong word in each sentence: a word, a phrasal verb or a modal. Nikita taps it and sees the correction. Aim: he explains in his own words why the word is wrong.

10 · Jeopardy

Nikita picks a cell, you read the question; the second tap shows the answer, the third closes the cell. The Cases category uses the facts from the background on the first screen.

11 · Speed Game

Read the definition.
Tap the word within 15 seconds.
No hints: recall the meanings.
15 seconds per answer. If time runs out, the right answer is shown. The aim is to recall the meanings without hints.
